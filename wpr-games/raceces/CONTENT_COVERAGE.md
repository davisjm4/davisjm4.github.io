## Language-quality audit

Performed by Claude (the author of the content) reading every response model (restatement, answer, both explanations, summary) and sample generated Missing Piece challenges. **No independent educator or subject-matter review has been done.**

**Problems found and fixed during the audit**

- Eight restatements gave away or partly stated the answer (for example, a restatement that already said "comes with a cost"). They were rewritten so the restatement only turns the question into a statement.
- The summaries opened with a transition ("Overall", "In short") in 28 of 30 models, which would let students identify the summary by its opening words. Twenty summaries and their new-information distractors were rewritten without a transition; ten keep one.
- Evidence sentences were plain statements everywhere, so "no attribution words" would have identified evidence. Ten evidence sentences now carry a natural attribution ("The passage notes…", "According to the text…"), and explanations use a mix of "Because/Since" openings (about 28%) and other forms.
- One explanation said "all three ingredients" while the claim named two; it now names iron, oxygen, and water.
- The first content validator run caught four evidence sentences that drifted from their source facts; they were re-anchored to the source wording.

**Remaining known weaknesses**

- Correct Missing Piece options are rarely the longest option when the missing sentence is a Restate (0 of 312) or a Summary (20 of 308): restatements are naturally short and the new-information summary distractor is naturally longer. Length-balancing steering removes most of the bias elsewhere (see the diversity report) but cannot remove it for these two roles without rewriting content.
- In Identify the Parts, the Restate, Answer, and Summary targets can be found by position because the response is displayed in order. Evidence and explanation targets are disambiguated by topic, so students must read for function. Advanced prompts add contrast wording to push toward function.
- Distractor "relevance" (the off-topic fact does not support the claim) is a judgment authored per model. Some pairs, such as a binary-numbers colors fact beside a text-storage claim, are close enough that a student could argue they are loosely relevant; the correct option still fits the surrounding sentences more directly.
- Subject accuracy was checked from general knowledge only. Statements such as the Martian day length, the 1954 silicon cell, and the "about a quarter of ocean species" reef figure are widely cited but have not been verified against primary sources.

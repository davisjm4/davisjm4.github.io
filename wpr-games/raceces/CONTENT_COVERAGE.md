## Content rules enforced at build time

- Every evidence sentence overlaps at least 70% of its content words with its source fact (attribution words such as "the passage notes" are ignored).
- Every number in any sentence also appears in the topic's source text.
- Two distinct evidence facts and one off-topic fact (in the excerpt, but not supporting the claim) exist for every writing model.
- No sentence is duplicated inside a model; none ends without punctuation; no template placeholders remain.
- A scan rejects excluded vocabulary (politics, elections, religion, violence, crime, drugs, explicit material, and similar). Result: 0 hits.
- Excerpt word counts fall inside per-level limits (Beginner 70–130, Intermediate 90–170, Advanced 110–195).

## Language-quality audit

The new content was authored against the listed topic facts and checked with the repository content validator, exhaustive grading sweep, and representative generated challenges. **No independent educator or subject-matter review has been done.**

**Content changes in this expansion**

- Added 60 separately authored response models with new questions, claims, evidence pairings, explanations, and summaries (four per topic).
- Added one supplementary source fact per topic. These additions include a source URL in the content bundle for editorial traceability.
- Changed the generated repeat-evidence distractor to describe the misconception without copying the visible Cite sentence verbatim.

**Remaining known weaknesses**

- Correct Missing Piece options are rarely the longest option when the missing sentence is a Restate (0 of 1942) or a Summary (17 of 1942): restatements are naturally short and the new-information summary distractor is naturally longer. Length-balancing steering removes most of the bias elsewhere (see the diversity report) but cannot remove it for these two roles without rewriting content.
- In Identify the Parts, the Restate, Answer, and Summary targets can be found by position because the response is displayed in order. Evidence and explanation targets are disambiguated by topic, so students must read for function. Advanced prompts add contrast wording to push toward function.
- Distractor "relevance" (the off-topic fact does not support the claim) is a judgment authored per model. Some pairs, such as a binary-numbers colors fact beside a text-storage claim, are close enough that a student could argue they are loosely relevant; the correct option still fits the surrounding sentences more directly.
- The validator checks evidence wording against source facts, but does not independently establish that every explanation is a sound inference or that every distractor has only one defensible interpretation. Subject specialists should review the authored models before relying on them as a formal assessment.

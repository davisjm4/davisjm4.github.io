# Instructional content review

Author review: 2026-10-25. External educator/classroom review: pending.

80 distinct instructional frames assess different access constraints, consequences, intervention objectives, limitations and evidence relationships. Five operational clues or numerical conditions per frame compose 400 instances. Names and demographic substitutions are not used as diversity counts. The inventory covers all 20 requested contexts, 12 barrier labels and 12 solution categories. Repeated underlying concepts remain possible; this is not an unlimited story generator.

Review principles:
- Infer barriers from stated resources and observed constraints, never from rural/urban location, age, income, disability or language identity alone.
- Separate service coverage from affordable subscription; household ownership from availability at task time; task suitability from possession; capacity from reliability; task skills from language and interface support.
- Barrier dimensions can overlap. Questions ask for the supported distinction among the offered choices or a narrowly stated goal. Physical reach/hours and offered service channels are not claimed to be mutually exclusive universal categories.
- Solution cases state feasible routes, deadlines, compatibility, coverage, budgets or authorized support conditions. No specific real subsidy eligibility, construction promise or medical decision is asserted.
- Immediate access alternatives are distinguished from long-term infrastructure goals. Offline content still needs initial and final transfers. A device gift can leave recurring service and maintenance needs.
- Impacts are framed as supported risks/opportunities rather than inevitable losses, demographic traits or guarantees of equal outcomes.
- Every statistic is fictional. Evidence questions distinguish counts/rates, percentage points, matched cohorts, coverage/adoption, paired resource requirements, task completion, survey scope and causal limits.

Revisions before release: replaced irrelevant consequence/solution distractors with adjacent access needs; removed several obviously weak size/display suggestions; shortened substantive alternatives to reduce length cues; reversed several numerical comparison directions; added a single-barrier fallback when the two-barrier cooldown is infeasible.

Across 400 instances, 100 keys are longest (25.0%) and 105 shortest (26.25%), including ties. Term labels naturally differ in length. This is a descriptive audit, not proof of zero wording cues. Classroom review should assess distractor plausibility and reading load as well as alternative interpretations.

review-fixtures.js independently pins 16 key distinctions and rationales. tests.mjs independently checks the raw numerical facts in all 80 evidence instances, including integer cross-products for proportions and multiple possible overlaps for separate resource totals. Automated structural checks cannot prove all instructional judgments uniquely correct; author-reviewed scopes and the source definitions remain necessary. No external educator certification is claimed.

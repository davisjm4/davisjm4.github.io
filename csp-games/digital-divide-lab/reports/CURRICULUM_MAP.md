# Curriculum alignment and repository audit

Reviewed 2026-10-25. [Current AP Central course page](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/courses/ap-computer-science-principles) links the [official CED](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-computer-science-principles-course-and-exam-description.pdf), Effective Fall 2023. Primary alignment: **Big Idea 5, Topic 5.2, IOC-1.C** (printed course framework p.116): access differences, effects on individuals and groups, equity and actions by people, organizations and governments. Related impacts use IOC-1.A; evidence interpretation supports DAT-2.A / DAT-2.D. No source questions are copied.

The CED’s core digital-divide framing concerns access to devices and the Internet. Task suitability, skills, accessibility, language, support and sustained meaningful use are concrete supporting applications here; they are not all separately named AP learning objectives. [ITU meaningful connectivity](https://www.itu.int/itu-d/meetings/statistics/umc2030/) supports considering quality and affordable productive use. This is not a claim that one framework is the only definition.

Repository audit against fetched main at a337b3d2e293fb5ac8f86af6cf82fb1af1be0e68: no dedicated digital-divide directory existed. Computing Impact Lab includes broad access/impact explanation scenarios, Vocabulary includes the digital-divide term, and AI Ethics includes accessibility and stakeholder analysis. Their implementations remain unchanged. This lab adds focused barrier/goal distinctions and independent fictional-data interpretation rather than their entire activities. Reused the shared theme, session lifecycle, Summary and finite-generator/interface patterns without changing shared scoring runtime. The local CS Games catalog numbering follows its existing navigation taxonomy; official CED alignment is identified by the codes above, not inferred from those local labels.

| Barrier | Supporting distinction |
|---|---|
| Infrastructure (infrastructure) | The network facilities needed to connect an area or location are missing. |
| Affordability (affordability) | A service or device may be available but its total cost can prevent use. |
| Device availability (availability) | A working device must be available when the person needs it, not just somewhere in a household. |
| Device suitability (suitability) | A device must support the particular task, software and input requirements. |
| Connection reliability (reliability) | A connection that frequently drops may not sustain an essential task. |
| Bandwidth (bandwidth) | Available data capacity must meet the task’s upload, download and shared-use requirements. |
| Digital literacy (literacy) | People may need opportunities to learn and practice a particular digital task. This cannot be inferred from age, income or location. |
| Accessibility (accessibility) | Essential interactions and information must work with intended users’ access methods. |
| Language access (language) | Instructions and support need to be understandable in a language the user can use. Language differences are not proof of low digital skill. |
| Technical support (support) | Timely help, maintenance and recovery can be needed to keep existing technology usable. |
| Physical access (physical) | Distance, opening hours and reachable locations can limit access to shared facilities. |
| Service availability (service) | A usable service channel must actually be offered for the intended people and times. A working Internet link alone is not enough. |

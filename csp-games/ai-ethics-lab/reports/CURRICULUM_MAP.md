# Curriculum and source map

Reviewed 2026-10-25 against the official AP Central CED linked by the current course materials (Effective Fall 2023). Core alignment is Big Idea 5. These are contextual applications of the objectives, not a claim that every AI term is a separately required AP exam term.

| Objective | Alignment used here |
|---|---|
| IOC-1.A | Beneficial and harmful effects; different stakeholder perspectives |
| IOC-1.B | Unintended consequences |
| IOC-1.D | Bias in data, algorithms and computing innovations |
| IOC-1.F | Legal and ethical concerns, permission and responsible use |
| IOC-2.A | Privacy risks from collecting, storing, combining and using personal data |
| IOC-2.B | Protection and misuse of computing resources |

Sources: [Official AP CSP CED](https://apcentral.collegeboard.org/media/pdf/ap-computer-science-principles-course-and-exam-description.pdf), printed course framework pp.114–125; [NIST AI RMF](https://www.nist.gov/itl/ai-risk-management-framework), [trustworthiness characteristics](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/3-sec-characteristics/), [risk-management core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/). NIST RMF 1.0 is supporting guidance, not an AP exam syllabus or the only ethical framework; its site notes a revision in progress. No copied scenario bank or source quotations are used.

AI-specific examples (proxy labels, group error audits, synthetic-media provenance, model/tool boundaries, appeals and lifecycle assessment) extend the core objectives. Accessibility is mapped to effects of innovations; it is not automatically equated with the digital divide. Local CS Games catalog topic numbers follow its existing navigation taxonomy, which differs from official CED topic numbering. Stable CED objective codes below are the curriculum identifiers.

Learn / Help — evaluate a claim, not a stereotype

Ask: What is the system meant to do? Who is affected? What does the evidence establish? Which requirement matters for this question? A useful innovation can have benefits, costs and unresolved uncertainties at the same time.

These fictional scenarios have bounded questions with reviewed rationales. They do not settle every possible ethical choice. Different legitimate priorities may need discussion, and no single metric or ethical framework resolves every concern.

Three worked distinctions

Bias and error: A wrong answer is an error. A repeated difference in relevant errors across comparable groups can support investigating unfair outcomes. Different selection totals alone do not establish why they differ or which fairness goal should apply.

Privacy and security: Encrypting appointment records helps protect access. It does not by itself make an unrelated profiling use appropriate. Review purpose, necessity and meaningful choice as well as access safeguards.

Claims and evidence: A model’s 95% overall accuracy can conceal different error rates across groups. Specify the relevant fairness criterion, compare appropriate cases and errors, and consider their consequences. More data or a human sign-off alone does not guarantee fairness or safety.

Transparency makes relevant use and limits visible. Explainability helps people understand reasons. Accountability identifies who must answer for decisions and arrange remedies. Disclosure alone is not informed consent; attribution alone is not permission.

Create task preparation

These concepts support practice before the official task. You must develop your own program and submit program code, an individual video and an individual Personalized Project Reference (PPR). The PPR contains selected procedure/call and list storage/use code, without comments or course content. The selected student-developed procedure uses an effective parameter and an algorithm with sequencing, selection and iteration in its body.

Practice games do not replace the official task or the exam-day written responses about your program. This view shares skill evidence with the Big Ideas; nothing is counted twice. Read current official directions.

How evidence, levels and reviews work
Privacy, sources and practice limits

No accounts, backend, progress uploads, identifying fields, external AI calls or third-party analytics are used by this pathway. Ordinary page requests still reach the site host. Your export includes skill IDs, counts, practice dates and anonymous activity records. Optional identity fields from Session Summary are never copied.

Only a first submitted answer counts toward accuracy; opening a game, editing an answer, revealing a solution and retrying do not add mastery evidence. Existing daily topic totals may be brought in once as estimated aggregate evidence. Their recorded date is the session's last activity date, not an invented per-question date. New evidence starts when this adapter is available; past days already cleared cannot be recovered.

Algorithm Dance Lab is available for project practice, but its combined stage checks are not assigned to individual skills. Structured choices do not demonstrate authentic collaboration, original design or independent Create written responses. Game coverage has gaps, which are described on individual skills. No games are locked.

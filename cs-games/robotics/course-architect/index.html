How these checks work

Digital checks test specified dimensions. They do not model traction, controller skill, tape thickness, speed or drivetrain physics. Floor and obstacle checks cover the rectangular body with safety clearance at adaptive positions, with a conservative buffer between samples. Near-touching designs can fail conservatively.

Corridors have rounded endpoint/junction pads. Curves require a symmetric lane wide enough for the rectangular body; in-place turns require a circular rotation pad. Physical tape boundaries must include these pads. Crossing routes are allowed and need classroom review.

SVG diagrams preserve physical dimensions in millimeters. Browser paper printing is not calibrated: print at 100%, disable fit-to-page, and measure the scale bar. Annotation space extends beyond the fitted floor region.

Scale, measurements & engineering reference

A scale ratio 1:N means one drawing unit represents N of the same actual units. A larger denominator gives a smaller drawing. Drawing = actual ÷ N; actual = drawing × N. For 4 m at 1:20: 4 m = 400 cm; 400 ÷ 20 = 20 cm on paper. A 30 cm robot becomes 1.5 cm wide.

Exact conversions: 1 cm = 10 mm; 1 m = 1000 mm; 1 in = 25.4 mm; 1 ft = 12 in = 304.8 mm. Display rounding does not change stored dimensions.

A straightaway travels along the current heading. In-place rotation changes heading at one position. Curved travel follows a circular centerline: arc length = radius × angle × π/180. A 90° arc is πr/2; radius is not travel distance.

Criteria describe success, such as reaching the finish. Constraints limit the design, such as floor space, paper size and robot turning ability. Record a prediction, identify a measured problem, make a justified revision and test again. Build the real course with tape and verify every measurement, turning pad, obstacle and driving maneuver.

Curriculum: VEX designing and scaling · Understanding scale · Converting units · Robo Rally Challenge. The final challenge combines team courses in the same units and scale and starts/ends at the finish line; plan and verify that combined course separately.

# PR #49 Workplace Readiness repair

Status is draft. The measured banks leave all 30 proposed skills teacher-assessed. An automated release gate supersedes the earlier blanket educator-signoff proposal. Existing notes and Claude matrices are retained as historical evidence.

## Evidence integrity

The authenticated Worker issues a question from a fixed bank. A seven-day HMAC permit binds its content revision, displayed option order, authorized profile and class, and current credential generation. The issued payload omits the answer key and comparison feedback. On sync the Worker checks scope, signature, expiration and current version and derives the primary family, concept and grade from its own bank. It stores a response ID and score without copying question text. The profile and reasoning family determine a stable event ID. The existing D1 uniqueness constraint and scoped insert preserve the first committed response across simultaneous writes and repeated issuance. A replay with a changed response or bank version cannot replace the accepted first attempt. Credential-generation changes revoke outstanding permits. This requires no schema migration.

Saved online permits can be answered offline and synced through the shared profile outbox. The browser shows a pending notice until the service can process the answer. Invalid or expired queued records remain in the local unverified store. Ordinary local practice and the existing reporting path are available for guests and enrolled students. Legacy client-authored WPR node claims cannot earn new mastery credit. The change creates no retroactive grade, progression, or assessment edits.

A permit validates the question issued by the service. It does not attest to what an untrusted browser rendered or prevent a student from consulting external material, including the open-source question bank. The pilot uses eligible-profile practice events with zero node dimensions. A future automatic rollout must also verify progression replay and restart behavior with eligible issued families before applying a policy.

## Instructional validation and coverage

Run `node cs-games/pathway/audit/wpr-mastery/release-audit.mjs --check` to reproduce 18,000 seeded generated questions. The [machine-readable audit](RELEASE_AUDIT.json) lists 188 candidate families, disposition and defect signals, answer position, length and wording tallies, sampled contexts, and the minimum versus measured capacity for each skill. Randomized text and order do not create additional families. The optimistic upper bound includes every attributed family and the three repaired examples and still reaches at most 11 units. All 30 fall short of their unchanged 20- or 30-question minimum. Of the 188 historical families, 110 were retained for ordinary practice, 75 were flagged for exclusion from mastery, and three were replaced in the server-checked practice set. None of those categories implies automatic eligibility. The older gaps for 50 coverage/attribution skills, eight performed behaviors, four skills without primary content and two answer-length repair candidates remain described in the original audit.

Comparative review independently challenged the keys of the flagged scenarios. The replacement emergency item specifies immediate eyewash and a second person who can alert the supervisor. Prioritizing the phone is defensible if the procedure or available help changes, but under the stated procedure this delays rinsing. The contributions item gives an agreed attribution policy and an editable slide. Private thanks and team recognition are reasonable but leave the attribution error in the client-facing record. The written-procedure item names the approver and makes approval a prerequisite. Advance warning would be defensible under a different procedure. The repaired examples retain competing actions and explain the decisive facts after submission. Ordering items that specify independent steps can continue to accept all valid permutations. Claims about unstated priority, task dependencies, or intent were excluded from mastery. Structural flags and model judgments are screening evidence rather than a proof that a natural-language key is uniquely defensible.
